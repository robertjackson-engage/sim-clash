About the game

What is Sim Clash?

A quick, video-game-style golf match for a real launch monitor. Two players in one simulator bay take turns hitting real balls into short, dramatic holes. It is loud, quick and deliberately over the top: power-up balls, sabotage, sudden death in a stadium, an announcer. It was built by a dad to play with his son, and it is free.

Is it really free?

Yes. It is open source under the MIT licence: no price, no subscription, no account, no adverts, nothing to buy inside it. The coins and trophies in the game are a scoreboard, not money.

Does it replace my simulator software?

No, and it is not trying to. It is the game you start when someone at home, or a friend, wants a quick match, not a place to play eighteen holes or work on your numbers. There are no real courses in it. It uses the same port as GSPro, so you run one or the other, not both at once.

Who makes it?

One person, as a hobby: a dad with a simulator bay who wanted a quick match to play with his son. It stands on Open-Birdie: the original simulator by Rooj Rin (rroojrooj/Open-Birdie) and the fork by jordan-shilts (jordan-shilts/Open-Birdie) that Sim Clash is forked from. It is not affiliated with GSPro, Foresight Sports, Uneekor or any launch-monitor maker.

How many can play?

Versus and the Trick Shot Duel are for two. Party takes two to four, Bullseye one to four, and Solo and Practice are for one. Everybody shares the one bay and takes turns.

Can it be played online?

Not today. Everybody uses the same bay and takes turns.

Can I add my own holes, balls or game modes?

Yes, and you do not have to be a programmer. The whole game is open source and laid out so that an AI coding assistant can work on it: one file per hole, one list of balls, tests that say when something broke. Make it yours shows how, with example requests that were checked against the code.

How finished is it?

It is a beta, and an honest one. It has been played on one simulator bay with one launch monitor (a Foresight GCQuad). The installed game has not been tested on a second PC by a person yet. The player's guide in the repository keeps a list of known rough edges. Reports from other bays are the most useful thing you can send.

Setting up

Which launch monitors work?

Fewer than "anything that works with GSPro". The game listens where GSPro's Open API listens (GSPro Open Connect, TCP port 921), but GSPro reaches most launch monitors through connections built into its own connector, and those cannot be pointed at another program. What can feed the game: a maker's own Open API connector, the open-source launch monitors, community connectors, and Foresight GC units through a bridge built into the game. The Connect page lists every launch monitor we know about in four tiers (tested, expected to work, community, not supported yet), generated from the same data the game's connection wizard uses. One unit, a GCQuad, has been tested on a real rig so far. If yours works, or does not, please tell us.

Do I need a launch monitor to try it?

No. While no launch monitor is connected there is a TEST SWING button on screen; T opens the list of test swings. The game plays a sensible shot for wherever the ball lies, or one you pick, and treats it like a real one, so two people can play a whole match with the mouse.

Does it run on a Mac or on Linux?

The installer is for Windows only, which is where launch-monitor software lives. The game is an Electron app run with Node.js, so starting it from source on another system may work, but nobody on the project has tested that.

Why does Windows warn me about the installer?

Because it is not code-signed: there is no company behind the project to buy a certificate. Click More info, then Run anyway. The Install page explains it in full, and the alternative of running from source.

I have a Foresight unit and GSPro. What do I need?

Nothing more. If you have GSPro with a Foresight unit, open the GSPro connector (GSPconnect) and everything else is built into the game: close GSPro itself, start Sim Clash, open GSPconnect, hit a ball. It needs your own GSPro installation and licence, because the connector is part of GSPro. It is experimental and has been tested on one GCQuad. The Foresight section of Connect has the details.

Why does Windows Firewall ask about the game?

Because the game listens for your launch monitor on TCP port 921. If the launch monitor's connector runs on the same PC you can refuse: local connections still work. If it runs on another device, allow private networks only.

Does it need the internet?

No. The game makes no network requests of its own: the stages, their skies and the sound are installed with it, and shots travel over a local connection inside your PC. The one exception is yours to switch on: with your own ElevenLabs key the announcer can fetch spoken player names.

Playing

We are very different golfers. Is it fair?

Fairer than real golf, which matters when the two of you are not the same golfer. Putting is helped, tap-ins are given, a slice is softened, nobody loses a ball, and the stroke cap ends a bad hole quickly. Power-ups and tricks let the weaker player steal a hole. It is still decided by who hits the better shots, and every one of those helps can be turned down. And in the lobby each player can be given HELP, three levels of handicap: easier putting, an extra power-up, a forward tee.

A ball rolled off the mat and the game counted it. Now what?

If the swing was under 40 mph the game asks STRAY BALL? straight away: press U. Otherwise open the match menu (Esc) and choose Undo last shot. Either way the swing is taken back as if it never happened.

Can we play with one launch monitor and a left-hander and a right-hander?

The game itself does not mind who is hitting: it plays whatever ball data arrives. Whether your monitor can read both sides without being moved is down to the monitor. One wrinkle: the callouts assume a right-hander, so a left-hander's slice gets announced as HOOK CITY.

Is there a shot clock?

No. Nothing times or hurries a player standing over a real ball, and no sound repeats while you set up.

Can I turn the power-ups off?

Yes. In the match options, power-ups can be a loadout (the default), unlimited, or off. On a rocket hole everyone still gets the free Rocket ball on the tee, because the hole cannot be reached without it.

Troubleshooting

The game says no launch monitor is connected
  • Is another simulator open? GSPro and Sim Clash both want port 921 and only one can have it. Close the other one completely. The game takes the port by itself within a few seconds: no restart.
  • Is the connector started and connected? Start it, press its Connect button, and check its address and port against the ones the game's wizard shows (SETTINGS > LAUNCH MONITOR > SET UP MY LAUNCH MONITOR). The wizard's status light names the likely cause.
  • Does the connector insist on finding GSPro? Give it this PC's network address instead of 127.0.0.1. Why that works.
  • Foresight GC: GSPro itself must be closed, GSPconnect must be open and must see the launch monitor, and no other bridge program may be running. SETTINGS > LAUNCH MONITOR says what the built-in bridge is doing. See Connect.
  • The log. An installed game writes what it is doing to %APPDATA%\Sim Clash\logs\game.log. A line saying port 921 is in use means another program has the port.
The monitor is connected but no shot shows up
  • Check the connector's own window first: does it show the shot? If not, the problem is between the monitor and its connector, before Sim Clash.
  • In a match, swings are ignored while a full-screen intro or result is showing. Press a key to get to the tee.
  • In the menus a swing is not a shot: only the title screen reacts to one. Get onto a stage first.
Every shot goes about half the distance it should

Your connector is sending ball speed in metres per second. Start the game with the environment variable BIRDIE_SPEED_SCALE=2.23694. See Connect.

There is no sound

Press S: it switches the game's sound on and off, and it remembers the last choice. Settings has the volume. Then check that Windows is sending sound to the screen or speakers you expect.

It stutters, or the picture looks coarse

Graphics, in Settings, has Auto, Ultra, High and Low. Auto drops to Low by itself when it cannot hold the frame rate, which is what a coarse picture usually means. Close other heavy programs (a second simulator, a browser full of video), or pick Low yourself on a modest graphics card.

Windows will not let me run the installer

On the blue "Windows protected your PC" box click More info to reveal Run anyway. If there is no Run anyway, the PC blocks unsigned programs outright: Windows 11 with Smart App Control switched on does, and so may a PC managed by an employer or a school. Running from source is the way there.

A putt I barely touched went in

Putting is helped on purpose, because launch monitors read putts poorly. Solo and practice play Easy putting by default; a match plays Assisted, where a must-make putt can be missed. If that is still too generous, set putting to Real (SETTINGS > PUTTING, or the match lobby's OPTIONS), and shorten or switch off the tap-ins in Settings. The three levels.

Something else is wrong

Open an issue on GitHub. Say which launch monitor and connector you use, what you did, what you expected and what happened, and attach game.log if you have it.
